# Current Product Assessment

**Product:** RelateAI

**Assessment date:** 2026-07-11

**Scope:** Current React Native repository only. This is a product and business audit, not a design review and not a claim about unobserved customer demand.

## Executive verdict

RelateAI is a well-defended relationship-assistant **domain prototype**, not a usable or production-ready customer product.

The repository contains real, often careful behavior for contacts, recurring occasions, reminders, drafting, review, handoff, privacy, encrypted persistence, backup, and recovery. Those primitives support a credible product thesis: help a busy, privacy-conscious person remember important relationship moments and send a thoughtful message with less effort and no loss of control.

The current product does not yet deliver that thesis to an end user:

- The only active interface is an intentionally unstyled JSON command console. There are 141 catalogued commands, 17 conceptual screen routes, and only three production `.tsx` files. Normal users cannot discover or complete the modeled journeys.
- The name, fresh-install defaults, and onboarding advertise AI, but the checked-in production composition has no authenticated AI session. It produces local template drafts. Authenticated email, Google sync, and unattended automation are also statically unavailable.
- The implementation is much broader than the proposed narrow customer job: approximately 41,217 non-test TypeScript/TSX lines, 61 production domain modules, a 5,951-line command runtime, 141 public harness commands, and 120 test files. Gift advice, relationship-health scores, derived analytics, CSV export, bulk actions, widgets, shortcuts, style training, and release diagnostics arrived before a usable activation or daily loop.
- At audit start, there was no repository evidence of a defined primary persona, market validation, acquisition path, monetization model, opt-in product telemetry, retention data, or willingness to pay. The reset now proposes a persona, segment, and business model, but none is validated; business value remains hypothesized rather than measured.
- Source-level safety is stronger than product readiness. Signed build, real-device, and store evidence are explicitly external requirements, and the current app has no customer UI to validate.

**Recommendation:** rebuild the product surface, scope, and journeys from first principles, while selectively retaining the hardened domain and data primitives that survive the MVP test. Do not preserve a feature merely because it has code or tests. Do not rewrite reliable recurrence, confirmation, privacy, persistence, and recovery code without a product or technical reason.

The first shippable product should do four things exceptionally well:

1. Save or import a person and an important date.
2. Remind the user at a useful time.
3. Prepare a genuinely personal draft from explicit context.
4. Let the user review and hand it off safely.

Everything else must prove that it improves activation, successful relationship actions, retention, trust, or paid conversion enough to justify its design, test, support, and privacy cost.

## Assessment frame and scoring

At audit start, the repository did not define or validate a target segment. This audit therefore used the narrowest credible initial customer: **a busy, privacy-conscious mobile user who cares about a manageable set of personal relationships and wants reminders plus help composing messages, while retaining final control of every send**. The replacement SSOT now formalizes that person and an India-focused market wedge as hypotheses, not validated facts.

Scores are deliberately strict and reflect the current product, not only the presence of domain code:

- **B — Business value:** plausible contribution to activation, retention, trust, differentiation, or revenue.
- **U — User value:** strength and frequency of the customer problem solved.
- **UX — Usability:** current ability of a normal user to discover and complete the feature.
- **C — Completeness:** end-to-end implementation, integration, recovery, and release evidence.
- **S — Strategic fit:** fit with the four-job initial product above.

All scores are 0–10. `B/U/UX/C/S` is the order used below. A high completeness score does not rescue low user or strategic value.

Decision meanings:

- **Keep:** retain the capability and its core rules; connect it to the future product surface.
- **Improve:** preserve the job and useful primitives, but close important product or integration gaps.
- **Simplify:** reduce choices, states, or scope before exposing it.
- **Merge:** fold the value into a stronger journey instead of maintaining a separate feature.
- **Rebuild:** preserve the customer job, not the current product/application shape.
- **Replace:** the current mechanism or claim cannot deliver the intended outcome.
- **Remove:** delete from the initial product; reconsider only after measured demand.

## End-to-end workflow assessment

### Activation

**Modeled flow:** choose one of four goals → account/local choice → contacts → notifications → AI/style/channels → backup → finish → Setup Doctor.

**Reality:** a user must know JSON commands. “AI wishes” is not achievable in the checked-in production composition, while local account, style, channel, and backup concepts appear before the first demonstrated reminder. Internal notification and AI toggles can be true while OS/provider readiness is unknown.

**Required outcome:** within five minutes, add/import one person, confirm one date, choose a reminder time, and see the next occurrence. Permission prompts should be just-in-time. Drafting, notes, backup, and biometrics should follow demonstrated value.

### Remember an important moment

**Modeled flow:** add/import person → add/import/merge event → verify recurrence → prepare checklist → reconcile reminder plan → schedule native notification → deep-link into event.

**Strength:** recurrence, conflict, ownership, and reconcile logic are unusually careful.

**Break:** the workflow is spread across command families and six checklist steps. No customer can see whether a reminder is merely preferred, authorized by the OS, planned internally, or scheduled natively. This is the strongest candidate for the first vertical product slice.

### Create and send a message

**Modeled flow:** choose person/reason → local template or provider draft → inspect context/variants → edit → acknowledge duplicate if needed → approve → wait for schedule → open destination app → return and confirm sent → optionally schedule follow-up.

**Strength:** review-first boundaries, stale approval checks, duplicate protection, route validation, and time-zone recovery protect trust.

**Break:** production has templates, not authenticated AI; scheduling does not mean unattended sending; handoff completion is self-reported; nine message states and many separate commands create extreme friction. The ideal customer flow is draft → review → open in chosen app, with rare exceptions handled inline.

### Maintain relationship context

**Modeled flow:** answer enrichment questions → add/pin memories → set tone/cadence/group → record gifts → view health/timeline/chat/analytics → act on neglected contacts.

**Break:** useful factual context is mixed with unvalidated scores, CRM-like data entry, and moralized “health/neglect” language. The product should save only context that improves a reminder or message and show factual history without judging relationship quality.

### Recover from failure or data loss

**Modeled flow:** Home/Activity flags issue → inspect route/provider → retry/revoke/reconcile; for data, export encrypted backup → confirm freshness → select/preview/restore with fresh unlock.

**Strength:** recovery and confirmation semantics are robust.

**Break:** the only interaction is JSON, provider-heavy states exceed the shipping composition, and local-only users bear manual backup/password risk. Recovery must be tested as a customer journey, not only as transitions.

## Broken, incomplete, or not production-proven

These are not future enhancements; they are present concepts whose intended outcome is currently unavailable or unproven:

- **No customer UI:** every modeled product screen is absent. Feature discoverability, comprehension, accessibility, navigation, interruption recovery, and task completion are unvalidated.
- **No production AI:** the secure endpoint/session abstraction exists, but the checked-in app cannot obtain an authenticated provider session.
- **No provider email:** delivery/reconcile logic exists, but authenticated provider delivery, sender verification, deliverability operations, and abuse controls do not.
- **No sync/account service:** Google sync is a type and unavailable branch, not a capability.
- **No unattended automation:** no durable worker sends messages; Smart/VIP modes only affect review prioritization.
- **No verified handoff delivery:** SMS, WhatsApp, and mail app handoffs cannot prove the destination message was sent or delivered.
- **No business measurement:** activation, reminder success, draft quality/editing, retention, privacy trust, support burden, provider cost, and paid conversion are not instrumented.
- **No monetization system:** there is no entitlement, purchase, trial, subscription, usage quota, or provider-cost control.
- **No completed production evidence in-repo:** signed builds, Android/iOS device smoke, and store submission evidence remain external by design.
- **No final accessibility evidence:** source contracts cannot substitute for screen-reader and dynamic-type testing of the future interface.
- **No broad-market release surface:** web is intentionally unsupported; this is acceptable only if mobile-only is an explicit strategy.

## Value, cost, and metrics

### What can be measured today

Only engineering proxies are available:

- 141 executable command contracts and 11 modeled command workflows.
- 61 production domain modules, approximately 41.2k production TS/TSX lines, and 120 test files.
- 5,951 lines in the central command runtime.
- Four explicitly unavailable capability families: sync, unattended automation, authenticated AI, and authenticated email.
- Zero repository-backed customer activation, retention, revenue, willingness-to-pay, or task-success evidence.

These numbers show implementation breadth and defensive testing. They do **not** show product value.

### Cost model for every retained feature

Each feature adds some combination of:

- product/design states, empty/error/recovery states, accessibility, and localization;
- domain and persistence migrations, native-device matrices, and regression tests;
- permission/privacy disclosure, support documentation, and incident surface;
- provider/backend cost, abuse control, delivery operations, and regulatory exposure;
- cognitive load that reduces activation and makes the four core jobs harder to understand.

A feature should remain only when its measured incremental value exceeds this full lifecycle cost. Code already written is a sunk cost, not a reason to ship it.

### Minimum product scorecard

Instrument this with privacy-preserving, explicit opt-in product analytics before expanding scope. Event payloads must exclude names, message bodies, notes, phone/email values, and event labels.

The numbers below are **illustrative strawman thresholds, not approved targets**. Phase 0 must establish denominators, segments, device constraints, and baselines; the product owner must then accept, revise, or delete each threshold before it becomes a release or investment gate.

| Outcome              | Provisional strawman for baseline planning                                                                                                        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Activation           | ≥60% of new users create/import one person, confirm one occasion, and create a working reminder within 5 minutes.                                 |
| Reminder reliability | ≥99% of eligible, authorized plans reconcile to the intended native notification; wrong-date incidents are release blockers.                      |
| Reminder usefulness  | ≥35% of delivered reminder opens lead to draft, handoff, snooze, or intentional dismissal within 24 hours.                                        |
| Draft efficiency     | Median time from reminder to reviewed handoff <60 seconds; measure edit distance and abandonment, not merely generation count.                    |
| Completion           | ≥60% of started draft reviews reach an opened handoff; self-confirmed send must be reported separately from verified delivery.                    |
| Retention            | Establish cohort baseline, then require ≥25% day-30 retention before adding secondary engagement features.                                        |
| Trust                | Zero known wrong-recipient/private-context leaks; duplicate-send attempts <0.1%; all such incidents receive severity-one review.                  |
| Data durability      | ≥99% successful encrypted backup/restore in supported device test matrix; destructive recovery is never the normal path.                          |
| Quality              | Crash-free sessions ≥99.8%; provider/template failure and fallback rates visible by release.                                                      |
| Unit economics       | Before paid AI, define per-active-user generation budget and require variable provider cost to remain below 10% of net subscription revenue.      |
| Support cost         | Track setup, permission, reminder, backup, and handoff issues per 100 active users; no feature graduates while its support trend is uncontrolled. |

## Scope reset

### Retain as the reusable core

- Person records, source identity, selective import, safe lifecycle, and conflict preservation.
- Local-calendar occasions, recurrence, leap-day policy, and conflict-aware event lifecycle.
- Reminder planning, native reconciliation, permission truth, and time-zone safety.
- Local templates/manual composer, context policy, review, duplicate guard, approval expiry, and explicit handoff.
- Factual notes/memories, minimal tone/language preferences, and a unified person timeline.
- Encrypted persistence, preview/confirm boundaries, redaction, backup/restore, clear-data recovery, and operation safety.

### Rebuild or materially improve

- Activation, Today, Moments, People, contextual Compose/Review, Settings, diagnostics, and every customer-facing workflow.
- The AI proposition: either productionize it honestly or remove AI from brand/onboarding/defaults.
- Reminder truth states and handoff terminology.
- Privacy disclosure for provider-eligible context and opt-in business telemetry.
- Runtime decomposition after the reduced product contract is stable.

### Remove from the first product

- Relationship-health scores, “neglected” labels, relationship analytics, analytics sharing, and CSV analytics export.
- Smart/VIP approve, fully automatic concepts, and bulk message actions.
- Gift advisor/budgets, standalone Style Coach, and separate chat history.
- Provider email, Google account/sync affordances, launcher shortcuts, and widget until measured demand justifies them.
- Separate setup wizard/doctor/activity destinations where an inline actionable fix is sufficient.
- Broad event taxonomy and advanced per-group/per-contact policy controls.

## Rebuild versus preserve decision

Do **not** rebuild the entire codebase from scratch. That would discard tested correctness in dates, lifecycle impacts, scheduling guards, confirmation tokens, privacy boundaries, persistence, and recovery without solving the actual problem: absence of a focused, validated product.

Do rebuild the product contract from scratch:

1. Define the primary customer, urgent job, promise, and non-goals.
2. Specify one vertical journey from fresh install to a working reminder and reviewed handoff.
3. Map only the domain services needed by that journey; delete or quarantine the rest.
4. Build the future Figma UI against those narrow application services, not the 141-command harness.
5. Add privacy-preserving outcome instrumentation and real-device evidence.
6. Expand only when activation, reliability, retention, trust, and economics clear explicit gates.

This is a **product and application-layer rebuild with selective core salvage**, not a visual reskin and not an indiscriminate technical rewrite.
